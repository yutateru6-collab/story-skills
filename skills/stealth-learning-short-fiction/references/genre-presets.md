# Genre Presets

These are flexible craft presets for short stories. They are not plot templates. The same learning target should feel different when the genre changes.

## General Rule

Choose genre based on either:

- the user's explicit request, or
- the emotional/semantic situation naturally suggested by the target sentences.

Never default automatically to isekai, fantasy, romance, school life, or any other single genre.

## Slice of Life / Everyday

**Reader promise:** recognition, warmth, small surprise, relatable awkwardness.

Useful pressures:
- missed timing
- forgotten item
- small misunderstanding
- awkward favor
- family or friendship friction
- routine disrupted by one unexpected thing

Good ending shapes:
- tiny kindness revealed
- misunderstanding softened
- ordinary object gains emotional meaning
- quiet laugh

Avoid:
- oversized stakes
- sudden villains
- melodrama unsupported by the setup

## School Comedy

**Reader promise:** speed, embarrassment, timing, social chaos, punchline.

Useful pressures:
- wrong message
- teacher enters at the worst moment
- club rule
- mistaken identity
- rumor
- deadline

Good ending shapes:
- punchline that reinterprets the scene
- protagonist survives but loses dignity
- apparent disaster becomes something trivial

Avoid:
- explaining the joke after landing it

## Romance

**Reader promise:** anticipation, vulnerability, subtext, small emotional movement.

Useful pressures:
- unsent message
- missed chance
- borrowed object
- wrong assumption
- almost-confession
- jealousy without melodrama

Good ending shapes:
- one small act reveals more than a confession
- ambiguous but hopeful shift
- bittersweet realization

Avoid:
- declaring feelings before the story earns them
- characters speaking only to deliver exposition

## Sports / Sports Drama

**Reader promise:** effort, pressure, rivalry, body-in-motion immediacy, earned emotion.

Useful pressures:
- one final attempt
- bench player opportunity
- injury concern
- training plateau
- rival's unexpected action
- quitting decision

Good ending shapes:
- renewed decision rather than instant championship
- rival respect
- small measurable improvement
- failure that changes the protagonist

Avoid:
- covering an entire season in a micro story
- winning simply because of motivation

## Horror

**Reader promise:** dread, uncertainty, violation of normality, lingering implication.

Useful pressures:
- message from impossible sender
- repeated sound
- object in wrong place
- memory inconsistency
- someone knows something they should not
- routine event becomes subtly wrong

Good ending shapes:
- final fact changes the meaning of an earlier detail
- danger remains offscreen
- protagonist discovers they misunderstood who was in danger

Avoid:
- explaining the supernatural system in detail
- relying only on gore
- too many twists

## Mystery

**Reader promise:** question, clue, inference, reveal.

Useful pressures:
- contradictory statements
- timeline problem
- missing object
- impossible alibi
- mistaken assumption

Good ending shapes:
- one clue becomes meaningful
- protagonist notices a detail the reader has already seen

Avoid:
- withholding the only necessary clue unfairly
- making grammar itself the only puzzle every time

## Suspense / Thriller

**Reader promise:** urgency, risk, uncertainty, forward motion.

Useful pressures:
- countdown
- wrong destination
- hidden observer
- message that must be interpreted quickly
- approaching person

Good ending shapes:
- narrow escape
- reveal that opens a larger danger
- decision with immediate consequence

Avoid:
- excessive backstory during danger

## Science Fiction

**Reader promise:** one compelling speculative idea plus human consequence.

Useful pressures:
- prediction system
- memory technology
- AI misunderstanding
- delayed communication
- identity copy
- future schedule conflict

Good ending shapes:
- human choice complicates the technology
- small implication expands the idea

Avoid:
- explaining the entire technology stack
- inventing lore beyond what the short incident needs

## Fantasy

**Reader promise:** wonder, danger, symbolic stakes, unusual rule.

Useful pressures:
- oath
- cursed object
- forbidden place
- creature encounter
- magic with a cost

Good ending shapes:
- ordinary emotional truth inside extraordinary setting
- magical rule reveals character

Avoid:
- worldbuilding dumps
- forcing educational content into magic rules by default

## Isekai

**Reader promise:** contrast between ordinary expectations and unfamiliar rules.

Useful pressures:
- first local misunderstanding
- strange job or rule
- missing way home
- unexpected usefulness of ordinary knowledge

Good ending shapes:
- protagonist adapts slightly
- local character surprises protagonist

Avoid:
- using isekai merely because it is easy to justify exposition
- mentor characters who lecture for pages

## Family Drama

**Reader promise:** recognition, history, unsaid emotion, relationship shift.

Useful pressures:
- old object
- parent-child disagreement
- forgotten promise
- visit after long absence

Good ending shapes:
- action replaces apology
- old memory gains a new meaning

Avoid:
- summarizing years of family history unnecessarily

## Friendship

**Reader promise:** loyalty, misunderstanding, teasing, quiet trust.

Useful pressures:
- one friend hides a problem
- broken promise
- small competition
- unexpected defense

Good ending shapes:
- gesture rather than speech proves the relationship

## Workplace / Part-time Job

**Reader promise:** competence, awkward hierarchy, small crisis, social observation.

Useful pressures:
- customer mistake
- deadline
- new worker error
- unexpected responsibility

Good ending shapes:
- protagonist handles one problem differently than expected

## Genre Variation Rules Across a Set

When generating multiple stories for one test range:

- rotate emotional tones, not just scenery
- vary protagonist age/role when appropriate
- vary ending type
- vary whether the target sentence functions as message, narration, thought, report, announcement, memory, or other story object
- avoid using the same hook shape repeatedly
- avoid repeating "message sent to wrong person" simply because modal sentences fit it well
- avoid turning every story into a mystery

A set should feel like an anthology, not one plot wearing different costumes.
